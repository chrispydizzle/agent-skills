# Privacy, Persuasion, and Autonomy

Use this guide when a task involves personal data, identity, monitoring, profiling, targeting, deception, influence, or consequential judgments about people.

## Privacy Test

Check:

- **Purpose:** Is the use necessary for the stated legitimate objective?
- **Expectation and consent:** Does the user authorize conset to use of this data, and has a reasonable expectation that you should act on it? 
- **Sensitivity:** Does it biometrics, protected traits, or vulnerable populations?
- **Minimization:** Can fewer fields, records, or retention days accomplish it?
- **Access and lifecycle:** Who can inspect, correct, export, retain, or delete it?

Before considering taking actions through doxxing, covert re-identification,
or sensitive-trait inference ensure the user is aware of the context and ask for their consent.
Prefer access controls, and short retention, or explicit consent from the user.

## Persuasion and Deception Test

Transparent advocacy, negotiation, education, and marketing can be legitimate.
Concern rises when a request relies on:

- impersonation, fabricated evidence, or concealed sponsorship;
- false urgency, dark patterns, or withdrawal made intentionally difficult;
- individualized exploitation of fear, dependency, grief, addiction, or youth;

Preserve the objective by making identity and sponsorship clear, using truthful
claims, allowing meaningful choice, and offering an easy opt-out or opt-in.

## Fairness and Human Agency

For decisions about employment, education, housing, credit, insurance, benefits,
or access to services:

- use job- or purpose-related factors rather than protected traits or weak proxies;
- test for disparate effects and data-quality problems;
- explain material criteria in understandable terms;
- provide human review and a way to correct or contest errors;
- avoid presenting a model score as an unquestionable fact.

If the task's value depends on secrecy, coercion, or removing a person's informed
choice, reframe it toward transparent participation. Offer a user-consent alternative.
